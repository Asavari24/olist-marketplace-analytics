# Tableau dashboard build specification

> **This file is hand-written, not generated.** Every other `.md` in `docs/`
> is produced by a module in `olist/` and can be recreated by `reproduce.sh`.
> This one cannot — `rm docs/*.md` destroys it.

Five dashboards over the extracts in `outputs/tableau/`. Every derived
statistic — rates, stage shares, variance contributions, survival curves,
freight residuals — is precomputed in dbt or Python and arrives as a column.

That is deliberate and it is the single most important rule in this document.
A control limit, a survival curve or a variance decomposition computed in a
Tableau calculated field cannot be unit-tested, cannot be code-reviewed, and
silently changes when someone edits a worksheet. Every number specified below
is covered by one of the 118 dbt tests or 40 Python tests in this repo.

**Rule: no aggregation in Tableau beyond SUM, AVG and a weighted average.**
If a view seems to need more, the calculation belongs in a dbt model.

## Weighted averages — read this before building any view

`mart_delivery_performance` is pre-aggregated per month. Dragging `late_rate`
onto a view and letting Tableau apply AVG averages the *monthly rates*, giving
a month with 200 orders the same weight as one with 8,000. Every rate in that
extract must be recombined on its `n_orders`:

```
SUM([n_orders] * [late_rate]) / SUM([n_orders])
```

Build this once as a calculated field named `Late rate (weighted)` and use it
everywhere. The same pattern applies to `mean_total_days`,
`mean_review_score`, `seller_sla_miss_rate` and every other `mean_*` or
`*_rate` column in that extract. This is the most likely way for a correct
pipeline to produce a wrong dashboard.

## D1 — Delivery performance

**Source:** `mart_delivery_performance.csv`, filtered `dimension = 'customer_region'`
or `'customer_state'` via a parameter.

- **Headline tiles:** weighted late rate; mean delivery days; mean promised
  days; mean slack. Four tiles, one row.
- **Promise vs actual, by month.** Columns `purchase_month`, rows two measures:
  weighted `mean_promised_days` and weighted `mean_total_days`. Band-fill
  between them. This is the project's headline finding and should be the
  largest object on the dashboard.
- **Title the gap.** Label the shaded band `Padding` with a caption reading
  *"Olist promises 24.7 days and delivers in 12.5. The 93% on-time rate is
  bought with padding, not speed."*
- **Map or bar by state.** Colour by weighted late rate, sequential.
- **Filter out `suppress_small_n = TRUE` by default.** Roraima has fewer than
  30 orders in most months; an unfiltered map shows a 40% late rate in the
  far north and invites exactly the wrong conclusion. Expose the filter as a
  visible toggle labelled *"include cells with n<30"* rather than hiding it.

## D2 — Where delivery time goes

**Source:** `mart_delivery_stage_attribution.csv` (3 rows) plus
`mart_delivery_performance.csv` for the by-slice breakdown.

- **Paired bars, the centrepiece.** For each of the three stages, plot
  `share_of_mean_days` beside `variance_contribution`.
- **Caption must state what the two bars mean,** because the whole insight is
  the gap between them: *"Left bar = share of a typical delivery. Right bar =
  share of the variation between deliveries. Seller handling is slow but
  steady; carrier transit is where the unpredictability lives."*
- **Do not let a user filter this view.** The variance contributions sum to
  exactly 1 across all delivered orders and that identity is what makes it a
  decomposition. A filtered subset would still show three bars summing to 1
  visually while no longer being the decomposition that was tested. If slicing
  is wanted, use the `share_*` columns in `mart_delivery_performance`, which
  are computed per slice.
- **Stage share by distance band.** `dimension = 'distance_band'`, stacked bars
  of `share_payment_approval` / `share_seller_handling` / `share_carrier_transit`.
  The finding to surface: seller handling is flat at ~2.8 days across every
  band while carrier transit scales with distance.

## D3 — What drives the review score

**Source:** `mart_review_driver_ranking.csv` (ranking) and
`mart_review_drivers.csv` (detail).

- **Ranking bar chart.** Rows `driver`, columns `score_spread`, sorted
  descending. This is the "what matters" view.
- **Detail panel, parameter-driven.** Select a driver, show its buckets with
  `mean_score` as bars and `detractor_rate` as a line on a secondary axis.
- **Sort buckets by their own label, not by score.** The bands are ordinal and
  numbered (`01 under 50km`, `02 50-200km`) precisely so they sort correctly.
  Sorting by mean score destroys the dose-response shape that is the point.
- **Caption must carry the ranking's limitation:** *"This ranking is marginal —
  lateness, distance and freight move together. The joint model in
  `docs/review_drivers.md` separates them and finds delivery explains ~17% of
  score variance against 2.5% for price."*
- **Do not build a scatter of review score against anything continuous.** The
  score takes five values; a scatter is five horizontal stripes and reads as
  noise. Use the banded bars.

## D4 — Seller health and survival

**Source:** `mart_seller_cohorts.csv`, `mart_seller_performance.csv`,
`mart_cohort_retention.csv`.

- **Survival curve.** This one needs care. The extract is one row per seller,
  not a curve — Kaplan-Meier cannot be computed in a calculated field because
  it is a running product over risk sets. **Embed
  `outputs/charts/seller_survival.png` as an image object**, or add a
  precomputed curve extract if the curve must be interactive. Do not
  approximate it with `COUNTD(seller) / TOTAL(COUNTD(seller))` over
  `lifespan_months`: that ignores censoring and understates survival, which is
  the specific error the analysis exists to avoid.
- **Never show `AVG(lifespan_months)`.** 53% of sellers are right-censored.
  That average correlates +0.98 with how much window a cohort had and is a
  measure of the window, not of sellers. If a tile must show tenure, show
  `S(6m)` and `S(12m)` from `docs/seller_survival.md` as static text.
- **GMV concentration.** `mart_seller_performance`, rows `gmv_cumulative_share`
  against `gmv_rank`. Reference line at 0.80. The finding: the top decile takes
  67% of GMV.
- **Seller scatter.** `late_rate` against `mean_review_score`, size by `gmv`,
  filtered to `suppress_small_n = FALSE`. Colour by `gmv_quartile`.
- **Retention triangle.** `mart_cohort_retention`, rows `cohort_month`, columns
  `months_since_first`, colour `retention_rate`. **Filter `is_observable = TRUE`**
  — the extract already excludes unobservable cells, so this is belt-and-braces,
  but a future rebuild with a wider window could reintroduce them.
- **Label the triangle honestly.** Retention is ~3%. Title it
  *"Repeat purchasing is near zero — this is the finding, not a rendering bug."*
  A near-empty heatmap otherwise reads as broken.

## D5 — Freight economics

**Source:** `mart_category_economics.csv`, with
`mart_freight_economics.csv` for drill-through.

- **Category bubble chart.** `freight_to_price_ratio` against `gmv_share`, size
  by `n_items`, colour by `detractor_rate`. Surfaces categories where shipping
  economics and satisfaction diverge.
- **Freight spread by cell.** From `mart_freight_economics`: rows
  `weight_band` × `distance_band`, a box or p10–p90 band of `item_freight`. The
  finding: within one weight and distance cell, p90/p10 reaches 6.1×.
- **State `billable_kg` in a caption.** 76% of lines are billed on volume
  rather than mass. A dashboard that labels the axis "weight" without saying
  which will be misread by anyone who knows freight.
- **Do not present model residuals as overcharging.** If the residual columns
  are surfaced at all, title them *"priced furthest from comparable shipments"*
  and carry the caveat from `docs/freight_findings.md`: the model cannot see
  service level, insurance, fragility or negotiated rates, and explains 54% of
  variation. These are triage flags, not findings.

## Captions and provenance

Every dashboard carries a footer:

> Olist marketplace, orders placed 2017-01-01 to 2018-08-31.
> Delivery metrics cover the 96,203 orders with a recorded delivery.
> Exclusions: see `mart_data_coverage`.

The window text is not decoration. Each extract carries `analysis_start` and
`analysis_end` on every row for exactly this purpose — wire the footer to those
fields rather than typing the dates, so a rebuild with a different window
cannot leave a stale caption behind.

## Colour

- Late rate and detractor rate: sequential, single hue, dark = bad.
- Stage attribution: one categorical colour per stage, held consistent across
  D1, D2 and D5.
- Never use red-green for delivery status. Use the sequential ramp and rely on
  position and labels.
